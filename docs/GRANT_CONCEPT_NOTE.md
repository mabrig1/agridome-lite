# AgriDome Lite Grant Concept Note

## Project title

**AgriDome Lite: Offline, Explainable AI Decision Support for Climate-Resilient Smallholder Horticulture in Nigeria**

## Problem

Smallholder horticulture farmers frequently make climate, irrigation and pest-management decisions with incomplete information. Extension support may be difficult to reach at the moment a problem appears, while many digital products assume continuous connectivity, high-cost sensors or advanced literacy. Crop losses, delayed interventions and inefficient input use reduce household income and local food availability.

## Proposed solution

AgriDome Lite is a mobile-first progressive web application that works offline for core record keeping. It combines manual climate logging, crop-stage guidance, pest-image decision support, yield estimation and a multilingual agriculture adviser designed for Nigerian conditions.

The proposed project will validate the product with farmers rather than treating software development as the final outcome. A structured pilot will test usability, retention, recommendation safety, data completeness and preliminary changes in harvest, pest loss, farm cost and income.

## Initial target group

Thirty horticulture farmers in one pilot area in Enugu State, with intentional inclusion of women, young farmers and participants with limited digital experience. Expansion beyond the initial location will depend on evidence, extension partnerships and local adaptation.

## Activities

1. Co-design consent, language and farm-recording workflows with farmers and extension officers.
2. Validate agronomic content and establish escalation rules for high-risk recommendations.
3. Train participants and field staff.
4. Collect baseline and twelve weekly outcome records.
5. Conduct usability interviews and recommendation-safety reviews.
6. Analyze results and publish a pilot report, implementation guide and scale-up plan.

## Expected outputs

- One validated, offline-capable pilot release.
- Thirty farmers recruited and trained.
- A de-identified longitudinal pilot dataset.
- A documented responsible-AI and data-governance process.
- A pilot evaluation report with a transparent limitations section.
- A protocol for a larger comparison study.

## Expected outcomes

- Faster response to observable pest and climate risks.
- More consistent farm record keeping.
- Improved farmer confidence in routine decisions.
- Preliminary evidence concerning harvest, crop loss, cost and income trends.
- Stronger collaboration between farmers, extension officers and technology researchers.

## Research and innovation contribution

The project will generate evidence about the feasibility of explainable, multilingual agricultural AI in low-connectivity smallholder settings. It will examine where AI advice is useful, where it fails and what human support is necessary for safe adoption.

## Sustainability model

Core farm records remain free during the pilot. Potential post-pilot revenue includes cooperative subscriptions, institutional dashboards, extension-service licensing, premium analysis, agribusiness partnerships and implementation contracts. Farmer data will not be sold, and any aggregated research or commercial use will require a clear lawful basis and appropriate consent.

## Partnership requirements

- Nigerian university agriculture or computer-science research group.
- State or local extension service.
- Farmer cooperative or producer association.
- Data-protection and research-ethics adviser.
- Independent agronomist for recommendation validation.

## Funding request structure

Budget should prioritize field coordination, farmer training, transport, participant communication, agronomic review, data management, product hardening, monitoring and evaluation, and dissemination. Hardware or sensors should be included only where required by the research design.

No application should claim clinical-style diagnostic accuracy, guaranteed yield improvement or completed field validation until the evidence exists.
