# AgriDome Lite — International Grant Readiness Pack
Version 1.0 | 16 October 2026 | Proposal stage, not a funded or validated project

## Investment case
**Title:** Climate-resilient horticulture through offline-first, human-supervised AI for Nigerian smallholders.
**Ask:** Indicative USD 75,000 for an 18-month feasibility and evaluation programme. This is a planning scenario, not a funder-approved budget.
**Lead:** To be confirmed; applicant eligibility, legal registration, governance, and financial accounts must be verified.
**Pilot:** 30 farmers in Enugu State; subsequent comparison study contingent on pilot outcomes.

## Why this matters
Smallholders face high information and connectivity constraints in pest, water and climate decisions. AgriDome combines local-first farm records, crop-stage guidance, AI-assisted photo triage, multilingual assistance and an extension referral pathway. The innovation to test is not AI diagnostic accuracy as an established fact: it is whether low-cost decision support is usable, safe, affordable and improves decision timeliness.

## Research questions
1. Is the tool feasible and acceptable to farmers with intermittent connectivity?
2. How complete and accurate are farmer-reported records across 12 weekly observations?
3. How often does AI advice require correction or escalation by a qualified agronomist?
4. What is the implementation cost per retained farmer, including training and support?
5. What sample size and design are justified for a future controlled effectiveness study?

## Work packages
- WP1 (months 1–3): institutional partnerships, governance, ethics review, accessibility and agronomic benchmark.
- WP2 (months 4–6): participatory co-design, localization, security/privacy remediation, field-team training.
- WP3 (months 7–11): recruit 30 farmers, baseline and 12 weekly observations, safety oversight.
- WP4 (months 12–14): analyze feasibility, data quality, cost and descriptive farm outcomes; independent review.
- WP5 (months 15–18): publish anonymized findings where lawful, extension toolkit, larger-study protocol and funding proposal.

## Results framework
| Indicator | Baseline | Target / measurement |
|---|---|---|
| Enrolled farmers | 0 | 30 adults with documented consent |
| Week-12 retention | Not measured | >=70%, weekly log |
| Required record completeness | Not measured | >=80%, data audit |
| Serious harmful AI recommendations | Not measured | 0 unresolved, independent review |
| Farmer support response | Not measured | Measured and reported; target set after co-design |
| Unit implementation cost | Unknown | Fully costed per active farmer |
| Agronomic recommendation accuracy | Unknown | Blind expert benchmark with uncertainty reporting |
| Crop loss / yield / income | Baseline required | Descriptive estimates only; no causal claims |

## Grant compliance and safeguards
- Do not start recruitment until relevant ethics and data-protection approvals are secured.
- Establish the legal applicant, finance controls, procurement policy, safeguarding/complaints process and anti-fraud controls.
- Confirm processor agreements, international data transfer conditions, AI image consent, retention and deletion processes.
- Separate product telemetry, farmer records and research datasets. No sale of farmer data.
- Publish an honest limitations register and negative findings, not just successes.
- Make the service accessible to women, low-literacy users and people with disabilities.
- Document adverse advice and provide human extension escalation.

## Two-sentence pitch
AgriDome Lite will test whether offline-first, human-supervised agricultural AI can improve access to actionable crop and climate guidance for Nigerian smallholders without requiring expensive sensors or continuous internet. An 18-month, locally partnered feasibility programme will generate transparent evidence on usability, safety, cost and the design of a future controlled evaluation.
